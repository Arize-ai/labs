TITLE: A young fern frond unrolling into a tight fiddlehead coil

IDEA: A fern grows by unrolling a coil that already holds smaller copies of the whole leaf, so one frond shows a structure repeating at every scale and a growing tip still uncurling.

COMPOSITION: The rachis (main stem) rises from the lower left at (128, 196). It arcs up and to the right through (170, 158), (210, 128) and (240, 110), then winds clockwise into a spiral crozier centred at (262, 92). The crozier is about 2.5 turns, with an outer radius of 30 tapering to an inner radius of 5. Nine alternating pairs of pinnae (leaflets) branch off the rachis between the base and the start of the spiral. Each pinna is a narrow tapered blade swept about 55° forward from the stem. Pinna length shrinks steadily from 44 at the base pair to 10 at the pair just before the coil, and widths go from 9 down to 3. Each pinna's edge is scalloped into 4 to 7 small rounded lobes, fewer on the smaller ones. Inside the coil, three or four tiny curled leaflet buds, each about 4 long, are tucked along the inner side of the spiral. The whole subject fits within x 100–300 and y 58–198.

LIGHT: The crozier is the brightest mass. Its outer turn sits at 0.8 and brightens to 0.95 toward the centre. The spiral carries a soft glow of radius about 55 centred at (262, 92), falling from 0.3 to nothing, as if the new tissue were lit from within. The rachis is 0.6, with a lighter upper-left edge of 0.7 and a darker underside of 0.45. The pinnae are mid-tones from 0.55 near the coil to 0.4 at the base. Each pinna is shaded across its width: its upper-left half is about 0.15 lighter than its lower-right half, which gives it a slight curl. The scalloped lobes fade to 0.3 at their tips. The buds inside the coil are 0.7. Everything outside the frond and its glow stays at the bare background texture, with no ground, horizon or second plant. The gaps between pinnae also stay dark.

LINES:

- Each pinna has a half-strength solid outline along its scalloped edge.

ORANGE: Only the innermost coil of the crozier is orange, the last full turn from a radius of about 11 down to the tip at (262, 92), in both its dots and its line. This is the frond's growing point, the newest and most tightly curled tissue, so the eye should land there first.

VARIATION:
1. Jitter each pinna's length by ±12%, its angle by ±6° and its lobe count by ±1, so no two fronds have the same rhythm.
2. Vary the spiral between 2.25 and 2.75 turns, and shift the rachis arc's midpoint by up to ±8 units, so each print shows the frond at a slightly different moment of unrolling.
