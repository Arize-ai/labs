TITLE: Two stars of a binary system circling one shared centre

IDEA: Love is two bodies that bend each other's paths until both orbit a point neither owns, and a binary star shows that without a heart.

COMPOSITION: The shared centre of mass is a point at (192, 125). Star A, the larger, is a disc of radius 32 centred at (160, 128). Star B, the smaller, is a disc of radius 20 centred at (268, 118), on the far side of the centre, so A, the centre and B lie on one slightly tilted line. Two concentric ellipses are centred on (192, 125), both tilted −8°. A's orbit has rx 32 and ry 12, so it passes through A's centre. B's orbit has rx 76 and ry 28, so it passes through B's centre. A faint bridge of light spans the gap between the two stars, from about x 192 to x 248, and is roughly 14 tall at its waist.

LIGHT: Star A is the brightest mass. Its core is tone 1.0, shading to 0.8 at the lower-right limb because the light comes from the upper left. It has a glow falling from 0.5 at its rim to nothing by radius 58. Star B has a core of 0.85, shading to 0.65 at its lower right, and a glow from 0.4 at its rim to nothing by radius 36. Where the two glows meet, the bridge settles at 0.25–0.3 and pinches thinnest midway, like a stream drawn from one star toward the other. The interiors of the ellipses away from the stars stay at background texture, and so does everything else on the card. Only the plain dot lattice remains outside the glows.

LINES:
- A's small orbit ellipse: half strength, solid.
- B's large orbit ellipse: half strength, dashed.
- Each star's outer disc edge: full strength, solid.
- A short line along the axis from A's centre through the shared centre to B's centre, about 120 long: faint, dashed, like an instrument's alignment mark.

ORANGE: Only the shared centre is orange, as a dot of radius 4 at (192, 125) with a faint thin orange ring of radius 9 around it. The eye should land on the empty point both stars answer to, which is the actual subject of the plate.

VARIATION:
- The orbital phase: rotate the two stars together around the shared centre by a random angle between −25° and 25°, keeping them exactly opposite each other on their ellipses.
- Add a slight random waviness to the width of the bridge and the falloff of the glows, so the stream between the stars never sits quite the same twice.
