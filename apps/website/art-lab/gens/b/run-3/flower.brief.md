TITLE: A sunflower head seen from above, its seeds in golden spirals

IDEA: A flower is nature's most legible piece of hidden order: a single bloom whose seeds sort themselves into exact spirals, beautiful at a glance and lawful when measured.

COMPOSITION:
- The bloom is centred at (200, 125).
- The seed disk is a circle of radius 30. It holds about 150 florets placed by the golden angle (137.5°). Floret n sits at radius 2.4·√n. Each floret is a small round mark of radius 1.2 at the centre, growing to 1.8 at the rim.
- A ring of 13 petals surrounds the disk. Each is a pointed ellipse 36 long and 14 wide. Each runs radially from radius 30 out to radius 66, evenly spaced, with the first petal pointing straight up.
- Behind the 13, a second ring of 13 petals is rotated half a step. These are slightly shorter, reaching radius 60, so their tips peek out between the front petals.
- There is no stem, leaf or ground. The bloom floats alone, like a specimen photographed straight down.

LIGHT:
- The disk florets are the brightest mass.
  - They are 0.9 at the outer rim, where the mature seeds sit.
  - They ease to 0.6 toward the centre, where the youngest florets are tiny.
  - The gaps between florets stay at background tone, so the spiral pattern reads in dots.
- The front petals run along their length.
  - Each is 0.65 at the base and 0.35 at the tip.
  - Petals on the upper-left side are lifted by about 0.1, and those on the lower-right are lowered by about 0.1, following the light.
- The back petals are a flat 0.3, so they read as lying behind.
- A soft glow surrounds the whole bloom. It starts at 0.2 just outside the petal tips and falls to nothing by radius 85.
- A narrow band at radius 28–31, where the petals meet the disk, stays dark at about 0.1. This makes the disk sit visibly in a shallow cup.
- Everything beyond radius 85 is left as untouched background texture.

LINES:
- The edge of the seed disk is a circle of radius 30, full strength, solid.
- Each front petal is outlined in half strength, solid.
- Each front petal has a midrib, a straight line from its base to about two-thirds of its length, faint, solid.
- Each back petal is outlined in faint strength, solid.
- One logarithmic spiral traces a single parastichy arm through the disk, from the centre to the rim. It is half strength and dashed, like a measurement overlay on a field-guide plate.

ORANGE: The eight or so florets that lie along the dashed spiral arm are printed in orange. The eye lands on the moment the pattern reveals itself: one spiral picked out from the crowd of seeds, which is exactly what careful observation of a system feels like.

VARIATION:
- Rotate the whole bloom by a random angle of up to ±20°.
- Jitter each petal's length by ±10% and its angle by ±3°, so the ring feels grown rather than drawn.
- Choose at random which of the visible spiral arms gets the orange highlight and the dashed overlay.
