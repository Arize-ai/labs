TITLE: A small refractor on a tripod, aimed at one bright star

IDEA: A telescope is an instrument that turns a single faint point of distant light into something you can examine. That is the essay's argument for observation: aim carefully at one thing and look closely.

COMPOSITION:
- **Tube.** A tapered cylinder whose axis runs from the eyepiece end at (128, 168) up to the objective end at (258, 92), about 150 units long and tilted roughly 30° upward to the right. It is 14 units wide at the eyepiece end and 22 units wide at the objective end.
- **Dew shield.** The last 28 units of the tube, ending at (258, 92), flare slightly to 26 units wide.
- **Objective lens.** A thin ellipse closing the front of the tube, 26 tall by 8 wide, centred at (258, 92) and set perpendicular to the axis.
- **Eyepiece.** A short stub 12 long and 7 wide that juts down and back from (128, 168), perpendicular to the tube.
- **Mount.** A small block of 14 by 10 units on the underside of the tube at its balance point, near (188, 140). A collar ring wraps the tube there.
- **Tripod.** Three straight legs from the mount down to the feet at (146, 192), (192, 194) and (236, 190).
- **Star.** A point at (300, 66), with a glow of radius about 34 around it.
- **Light rays.** Three converging rays run from the star to the top, centre and bottom of the objective lens.
- **Ground.** A short ground line runs from (120, 194) to (264, 194) under the feet.

LIGHT:
- **Star.** Tone 1 at its core, a 4-unit radius where the dots kiss. A glow falls off over the 34-unit radius to about 0.15, so the star sits in a soft halo of shrinking dots.
- **Tube.** Shaded as a cylinder lit from the upper left. The upper-left edge band is tone 0.7, the middle is 0.45 and the underside falls to 0.15. The tube also darkens slightly toward the eyepiece end.
- **Dew shield.** One step brighter than the tube where it faces the star, about 0.8 along its top edge. This suggests a catch of starlight.
- **Objective lens.** Tone 0.9, a bright lens face that picks up the glow.
- **Mount and collar.** Tone 0.4.
- **Eyepiece.** Tone 0.3.
- **Tripod legs.** Thin bands at tone 0.25, too narrow to read as more than texture. They are mostly carried by their lines.
- **Ground.** A faint pool of 0.1 under the tripod, about 60 wide.
- **What stays dark.** Everything else is left as bare background texture. This includes the sky between the star and the tube; only the rays cross it.

LINES:
- **Tube.** Full, solid outline of the tube's two long edges, the dew-shield flare and the eyepiece end.
- **Objective lens.** Full, solid ellipse.
- **Collar ring.** Half, solid band around the tube at the mount.
- **Eyepiece.** Half, solid outline.
- **Tripod legs.** Full, solid, one line each.
- **Ground.** Faint, solid.
- **Light rays.** Three faint, dashed rays from the star to the lens, converging as they arrive.

ORANGE: The star's core and its brightest inner glow, out to about a 10-unit radius, are in orange. The outer halo stays in the main ink. The eye should land on the target first, because the plate is about what the instrument is looking at. The dashed rays and the tube's angle then walk the eye back down to the telescope.

VARIATION:
1. The star's position should jitter within about ±10 units. The tube's angle should follow it, aimed between 26° and 34°, so the rays always meet the lens.
2. Scatter three to five tiny background stars across the empty upper half. Use tone 0.2–0.35, about one dot each, placed at random but kept at least 40 units from the main star and the tube.
