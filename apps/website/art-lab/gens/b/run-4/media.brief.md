TITLE: A light ray bending through a glass slab, refraction diagram plate

IDEA: A medium is what a signal travels through, and the moment a beam enters glass and bends is the clearest picture of a medium shaping what passes through it.

COMPOSITION: A flat rectangular glass slab spans x 130–270, y 95–165 (140 wide, 70 tall), centred at (200, 130). An incoming ray runs from (96, 62) down-right to an entry point at (170, 95) on the slab's top face. Inside the glass the ray bends more steeply, running from (170, 95) to an exit point at (200, 165) on the bottom face. The outgoing ray leaves from (200, 165) to (262, 193), parallel to the incoming ray but shifted sideways. A short vertical normal, 40 long, is centred on the entry point (170, 75 to 170, 115). A second normal is centred on the exit point (200, 145 to 200, 185).

LIGHT:
- Slab body: tone 0.35 at the upper-left corner, shading evenly down to 0.2 at the lower-right, so it reads as a solid lit from the upper left.
- Slab top face: a narrow band 4 units deep along y 95–99 at tone 0.55, catching the light.
- Rays: tone 1.0 along a 3-unit-wide core. Each ray has a tight glow falling from 0.6 to 0 over about 10 units on either side.
- Entry and exit points: a small bloom of radius 8 at tone 1.0, fading out by radius 16, where the light strikes the surfaces.
- Background: everything outside the slab and the ray glows stays untouched base texture.

LINES:
- Slab outline (all four edges): full, solid.
- Inner edge of the top-face highlight band: faint, solid.
- Incoming and outgoing rays: full, solid, drawn along their centres.
- Both normals: half, dashed.
- The ray's undeviated path through the block, from (170, 95) continuing at the original angle to the bottom face near (225, 120)… clipped at the slab edge: faint, dashed. This shows how far the medium pushed the light aside.

ORANGE: The ray segment inside the glass, from (170, 95) to (200, 165), together with its glow. This bend is the whole point, the place where the medium acts on the message, so the eye should land on it first.

VARIATION: Vary the angle of the incoming ray by up to ±4°, recomputing the internal bend and the parallel exit ray to match. Also vary the slab's tonal shading by about ±0.05, so each print catches the light slightly differently.
