TITLE: A snake sliding forward out of its own shed skin

IDEA: A snake is most itself when it leaves an exact hollow record of the body it just had, a perfect trace of where it has been, while its tongue tastes the air ahead.

COMPOSITION: One long S-curve, seen from directly above, runs left to right across the card. The spine is a smooth curve through (96, 182), (140, 150), (180, 160), (220, 138), (250, 108) and the snout at (296, 94).
  - The left third, from (96, 182) to about (172, 156), is the shed skin. It is a hollow tube about 16 wide, slightly crumpled, and its open end flares to about 20 wide at (172, 156).
  - The rest, from (176, 154) to the snout, is the living snake. Its body is about 18 wide where it leaves the skin, swells to 22 around (220, 138) and narrows into a spade-shaped head about 26 long and 18 wide, centred near (282, 98) and pointing up and to the right.
  - A forked tongue extends about 14 units from the snout, splitting into two prongs about 5 long each.

LIGHT:
  - **Living body:** shaded as a round tube lit from the upper left. The upper-left flank is at 0.85, the centre ridge at 0.7 and the lower-right flank falls to 0.3.
  - **Scales:** a faint diamond pattern runs along the back, made by alternating 0.7 and 0.55 dot sizes in small lozenges about 6 units long.
  - **Head:** the brightest mass at 0.95, rounding off to 0.5 at its lower edge.
  - **Head glow:** a soft halo around the head of 0.2 at radius 28, falling to nothing.
  - **Shed skin:** very pale and papery, at 0.25 to 0.35, with a patchy dot texture as though translucent. Its interior reads as dark.
  - **Dark areas:** everything else stays background texture, with no ground plane and no cast shadow.

LINES:
  - Both edges of the living body and the outline of the head are full strength and solid.
  - A single centre spine line along the living body is faint and dashed.
  - Both edges of the shed skin are half strength and dashed, and the flared mouth of the skin is a half-strength solid ellipse.
  - A few short faint solid creases cross the skin to suggest crumpling.

ORANGE: The forked tongue, drawn as a full-strength orange line with two prongs and a tiny dot at each tip. It is the snake's sensing organ, the moment of reading the world ahead, and it sits at the leading edge of the whole curve where the eye arrives anyway.

VARIATION:
  - Vary the amplitude and phase of the S-curve slightly, so the body's bends and the head angle shift by up to about 10 degrees.
  - Jitter the placement of the creases in the shed skin and the diamond scale pattern, so each print's skin and scales are unique.
