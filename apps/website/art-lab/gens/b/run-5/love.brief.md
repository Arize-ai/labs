TITLE: Binary star pair orbiting one shared, empty centre

IDEA: Two stars held in orbit around a point that belongs to neither of them, each forever turning toward the other, stand for love as a centre that only exists between two people.

COMPOSITION: The shared centre of mass sits at (195, 125). A larger star, a disc of radius 20, is centred at (169, 125) on a small inclined orbit ellipse centred on (195, 125), semi-axes 26 by 12. A smaller star, a disc of radius 12, is centred at (265, 125) on a large inclined orbit ellipse, also centred on (195, 125), semi-axes 70 by 32. The two stars face each other across the centre, as real binaries do. A tiny mark of radius 3 sits at the centre (195, 125). Everything else is empty lattice.

LIGHT: The larger star is the brightest mass: tone 1.0 at its core, staying at 0.9 to its rim, with a slight lift on its upper left. A wide glow around it falls from 0.6 at the rim to nothing by radius 62, so the dots shrink outward through the full scale. The smaller star is tone 0.85 at its core and 0.7 at its rim, with a tighter glow falling to nothing by radius 38. Where the two glows overlap around the centre, they add to a soft mid-tone haze of about 0.25, a faint bridge of light between the stars that is brightest near (215, 125). The centre mark carries no main-ink tone. The rest of the card stays dark and untouched, especially outside the large orbit and in the corners.

LINES:
- Small orbit ellipse: half strength, solid.
- Large orbit ellipse: half strength, solid on its front (lower) half and faint dashed on its back (upper) half where it passes behind, to give the plane depth.
- Rim of each star disc: a full-strength solid outline, so both read at thumbnail size.
- Two short crossing ticks at the centre mark, each 8 units long, at plus and minus 45 degrees: full strength, solid, like an instrument reticle.

ORANGE: The centre mark at (195, 125), a small solid dot with its reticle ticks, both in orange. It is the empty point both stars answer to, and the idea lives there rather than in either star. Placing the only orange in the dark gap between two bright masses makes the eye settle on it.

VARIATION:
- Rotate the whole system's orbital phase by up to ±25 degrees. Both stars stay exactly opposite each other through the centre, so their positions shift along their ellipses together.
- Vary the tilt of the orbital plane, which is the minor-axis ratio of both ellipses, between 0.38 and 0.55.
